# Experiment 7 — F1 addendum: re-optimization under Experiment 1's own service rules

*Post hoc, 2026-10-04. **Not pre-specified.** It does not replace the
pre-specified F1 adaptive result in `EXPERIMENT7_CLOSEOUT.md` §5, which stands
as written. Artifact: `outputs/exp7/EXP7_F1_DECISION_SPACE.json` (sha256
`0d5e3da76ebdc6ad67493e4ce93061c1536e313ac558e605e9d631a764015280`; not
registered in `CANONICAL_RESULTS_v5.json`), generated by
`scripts/exp7_f1_decision_space.py`. That script reads only certified Stage 2
closure records and Stage 1 current-plan evaluations, and re-solves nothing.*

## Why this exists

Amendment §14.2 defines F1 adaptive as the F6-track N0 **REF** plan compared
with the N0 current plan at the same level. REF is the unconstrained cell of
Experiment 6's decision space, in which the optimizer may switch any
route-period OFF.

**Experiment 1 could not do that.** Its certified plans were solved under
`config/constraints.yaml`:

* `preserve_span: true`, so every route-period with baseline service keeps
  service;
* `policy_max_headway_min: 60`, with the ladder capped at max(60 min, baseline
  headway).

So the pre-specified Stage 2 comparison re-optimizes F1 in a **larger decision
space** than the one F1 was certified in.

The certified Stage 2 cell whose rules match Experiment 1's is **R1_H60**:
every baseline-served route-period stays ON, with headway at most max(60 min,
baseline). `EXPERIMENT6_CONSTRAINT_CATALOG.md` records that R1_H60 is identical
to R2 at s = 0 for exactly that reason. It was closed in the same F6 N0 closure
as REF. Same certifier, same fixed point. Same service rules as Exp 1; the
model instance and certifier are Exp 6's.

## The same REF cell has two fixed points

The N0 REF cell at each level was closed twice:

* on the F6 track (W + X transfers from the policy cells);
* independently on the F4 track (X transfers only).

Each run reached a certified fixed point. On five of the seven levels the two
fixed points differ in objective by more than 0.03%.

| level | F6-track REF F1 (OFF) | F4-track REF F1 (OFF) | F4 objective vs F6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| BASE | −5.43% (17) | **+30.48%** (48) | **−0.161%** (better) |
| A5_LAM1 | +181.72% (139) | +181.72% (139) | same plan |
| A5_LAM4 | −6.76% (12) | −6.77% (12) | +0.007% |

At BASE (λ = 2) the best-known plan **in the decision space that allows service
to be switched off** has an F1 of **+30.5%**. That plan comes from the F4 track
and is 0.16% better in objective. It switches 48 route-periods off and serves
about 17,350 modeled trips, against 21,091 for the F6-track plan.

This is Experiment 6's flat-objective finding (§7 of its closeout) appearing
directly in F1. Once route-periods may be switched off, plans within a fraction
of a percent of each other in objective differ by tens of percent in unserved
demand. **Unserved demand is not identified by the λ = 2 objective in that
decision space.** The pre-specified F1 adaptive value (F6-track REF) is a
basin-dependent figure, and Experiment 7 closeout §9 prohibits treating such
figures as findings in their own right.

## Reading

1. **Under Experiment 1's service rules (study safeguards in
   `config/constraints.yaml`; no documented COTA numeric standard), F1 survives
   re-optimization at every λ ≥ 2 level tested: −2.1% to −7.0%.**
   * At λ = 1 the R1_H60 result is +0.12%, a near-tie, technically a sign
     change.
   * R1_H30 holds even at λ = 1 (−4.56%).
   * At λ ≥ 2 this agrees in sign and range with the Stage 1 fixed-plan
     result (−1.9% to −7.0%). At λ = 1 it differs in sign from Exp 1's
     uncertified frontier point (−1.42%), which used a different model
     instance (crowding on, 243,257-path set) and solver (Gen1).
2. **When service may be switched off, F1 is not identified.** The two
   independent closures of the same REF cell reach F1 values from −5.4% to
   +30.5% at BASE. Every positive REF value coincides with 38–139 route-periods
   switched OFF. The R1 cells (no baseline-served route-period OFF) switch off
   none.
   * R3_SPAN preserves span only at period granularity, so some route-periods
     still go OFF (1–86). It sits in between.
   * Whether R1_H60's result is itself basin-stable was not tested by a second
     independent closure. Exp 6 found that R1 cells did not change basin under
     closure; in Exp 7, R1_H60 at BASE did change basin (a cross-level import,
     0.009% in objective), with F1 moving from −6.55% (Exp 6 plan) to −6.57%
     (post hoc). Exp 1's seeds, searching the same no-OFF space with a different
     solver, agreed on the aggregate within an SD of 0.064 percentage points (range 0.12).
3. **The objective, not the network, is what breaks.** Because a lost trip
   costs λ × 60 while a served trip costs its full generalized cost, the
   λ-weighted objective rewards shedding hard-to-serve riders whose generalized
   cost exceeds λ × 60. Service-preservation rules stop that from happening.
   This is a statement about the objective and about which service rules a
   planner chooses to impose. It is not a claim that the rules are costless:
   Exp 6 prices R1_H60 at +0.48% of the objective at BASE.
